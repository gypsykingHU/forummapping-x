Five stories from the past four days where geography is doing the talking: a Red Sea chokepoint changing hands in Yemen, a Gulf kingdom under Iranian drones, a NATO state shutting its land border with Russia, a peace idea that would redraw Ukraine's east, and a sea code that still isn't written.

**1. The Houthis are left holding the Bab al-Mandab.** Saudi-backed Yemeni government forces say they are moving troops away from the Bab al-Mandab Strait to defend the interim capital, Aden, the BBC reports, leaving the Iran-backed Houthis in control of the waterway. The New York Times calls it a setback for the government and Saudi Arabia, citing analysts who say momentum in the civil war is tilting toward the Houthis. Al Jazeera says the advance threatens one of the government's last strongholds, and separately reports that the Houthis unilaterally released 600 war detainees. The strait, between Yemen and the Horn of Africa, is the southern gateway to the Red Sea and the Suez Canal route.

**2. Iran strikes Bahrain twice in three days.** Iran launched drones at a US military base in Bahrain on Sunday, the Guardian reports, its second attack in three days in retaliation for US strikes in Iran. The Guardian says Iran's Revolutionary Guard claimed the first strike destroyed a US early-warning radar, in response to a US airstrike on a communications tower at Sirik, overlooking the Strait of Hormuz. The New York Times says the attacks resume after a two-month lull as US-Iran peace talks stall, and Al Jazeera reports a third US aircraft carrier has arrived in the region. Saudi Arabia, Qatar, Jordan and Egypt condemned the strikes on their Gulf neighbour.

**3. Estonia will shut its border with Russia.** Estonia will close all its border crossings with Russia from 1 December, the Guardian and Al Jazeera report, after an attempted attack on the drone maker Threod Systems. The Guardian says eight people were detained, that authorities said Russia's GRU military intelligence ordered the attack, and that the Estonian prime minister linked it to Tallinn's support for Ukraine. Al Jazeera describes it as an alleged arson attempt at a drone factory. Estonia's land frontier with Russia is also an external border of both NATO and the EU, so closing every crossing hardens one of the lines where the two blocs meet.

**4. A Donbas-for-NATO idea, and a European seat at the table.** Zelenskyy said the US has broached the idea of Kyiv giving up the Donbas region in exchange for NATO membership, and that any security deal could go to a referendum, Deutsche Welle reports. Separately, the New York Times reports that Europe has agreed on a six-member team to represent it in talks with Russia, after long seeking a seat, and that US envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner asked Vladimir Putin in Moscow to include Europe. Nothing has been agreed. But a trade of territory for an alliance would move both Ukraine's eastern border and NATO's.

**5. No deal yet on a South China Sea code.** ASEAN leaders and China fell short of agreeing a code of conduct for the South China Sea, Al Jazeera reports; the bloc also denounced violence in Myanmar and extended a ban on Myanmar's leader attending its summits. The sea is claimed in overlapping parts by China and several ASEAN members, and a code has been under negotiation for years. In a separate report, Deutsche Welle says new military facilities in Laos and Cambodia have smaller countries worried that China's military network in Southeast Asia is expanding. Each of these reports comes from a single outlet.

**The data behind it**

This map shows military spending as a share of GDP, using World Bank figures. Ukraine is in a category of its own at 39.6%, with Russia at 7.5%. Next comes NATO's eastern flank: Poland at 4.5%, Latvia at 3.6%, Estonia at 3.4% and Lithuania at 3.1%, all above most of western Europe, where France sits at 2.0%, Germany at 2.3% and Italy at 1.9%. Ireland spends just 0.2%. The shading deepens roughly with proximity to Russia, which is why Estonia's border closure (story 3) and the debate over where Ukraine's border will settle (story 4) are followed so closely in the Baltic capitals.
